Chapter 9

How to Read a Playbook

Twelve playbooks cover all 23 assignable roles. Several roles share a playbook because they share a desk — a Police, an Officer and a Metro see the same screens and do the same work; the distinction is rank, not permission.

9.1 Structure

SectionWhat it tells you
Who this isThe job, and which role values map to it.
ReachExactly which screens the route matrix opens for this role. Anything not listed redirects to /403.
Shift routineThe order to do things in, start to end.
TasksNumbered procedures. Full steps are in Volume V; the playbook says which apply to you and in what order.
Not yoursWhat this role deliberately cannot do, and who to go to.

9.2 Two rules that apply to everybody

Silo scope Unless you hold a global role, you see your own department's records plus anything routed to your department. This is enforced in the database, so it holds on every screen, every export and every search — there is no view that shows you more.
Everything you do is attributed Every mutation writes an audit row with your name, department and a description. This is not surveillance; it is what makes the record usable as evidence, and it is what protects you when somebody asks why a decision was made.

Call-Taker

IncidentLogger

The intake desk. Answers the telephone and turns a call into an incident record. Does not dispatch.

10.1 Reach

ScreenUse
/dashboardYour board.
/dispatch/log-callYour primary screen.
/incidents · /incidents/[id] · /incidents/mapLook up what you logged.
/reports · /report-hub · /reports/builderReporting.
/case-managerCase review.
/cameras · /cameras/footageCamera wall and retrieval.
/hierarchyReporting lines.
/roster/mine · /roster/allocationYour schedule and the duty board.
/leaveApply for leave.
/performance/mineYour own throughput.
/notifications · /profile · /search · /assistantUniversal.

10.2 Shift routine

  1. Sign in. Check the top bar shows your department and the alert bell for anything outstanding.
  2. Open Dispatch → Log Incoming Call and leave it open. The intake clock starts when the screen opens, so a fresh screen per call keeps the timing honest.
  3. Take calls (10.3).
  4. Between calls, check Incidents for anything you logged that has not moved.
  5. At end of shift, confirm nothing you captured is still sitting in the queue unrouted, and hand over anything unusual verbally.

10.3 Tasks

Log an incoming call
Screen /dispatch/log-call  ·  Writes incidents, audit_logs  ·  Full procedure 22.1
  1. Capture Caller name and Contact as the caller gives them. Do not correct spelling later at the cost of listening.
  2. Enter the Call description / narrative — what is happening, in their words.
  3. Set the location: type the address and take a suggestion, or drop a pin on the map. The two stay in step; you do not need to type coordinates.
  4. Classify: incident type from the configured category tree, then Region and Incident severity.
  5. If casualties: Number of victims and, for EMS, the Medical severity code (P1 Red through P4 Blue). Tick EMS not required if it plainly is not a medical call.
  6. Watch for the duplicate warning. If it names an existing incident, stop and check before creating a second one.
  7. Choose the receiving departments if you are permitted to route; otherwise submit and the dispatch desk will.
  8. Submit. You get the incident number in a confirmation.
Afterwards The incident exists with a sequential number, your name stamped as call-taker, and — if routed — an alert on every screen in the receiving departments and a push to their phones.
Attach something to a call at intake
Screen /dispatch/log-call  ·  Writes incident_attachments, storage
  1. Use the attachment control before submitting.
  2. Submit as normal.
If the upload fails The incident is still created and you are told the attachment did not upload. Re-attach it from the incident page — the call is never lost to a failed file.
Ask a caller for their GPS position
Screen /incidents/[id]  ·  Needs BulkSMS configured  ·  Full procedure 22.5
  1. Open the incident.
  2. Enter the caller's mobile number in Contact citizen and submit.
  3. They receive an SMS with a single-use link. When they open it and allow location, the position lands on the incident.
Use when The caller cannot describe where they are — common on freeways and in informal settlements.

10.4 Not yours

Change an incident's statusThe attending crew, or the dispatch desk.
Close a caseCase manager or supervisor.
Open a department's reportsThat department.
Chapter 11

Control Room Operator

ControlRoom · Operator

The full control-room desk: takes calls and dispatches. Global silo scope — sees every department.

11.1 Reach

Everything the call-taker reaches, plus the whole dispatch desk: /dispatch/pending, /dispatch/assign/[id], /dispatch/kpi, /dispatch/ptt, plus /disaster. Not the departmental report sections, and not /admin.

11.2 Shift routine

  1. Sign in and clear the alert bell — read anything raised overnight, including the morning digest.
  2. Open Dispatch → Pending Queue. The figure at the top is your queue depth; it is live.
  3. Work the queue oldest-and-worst first. Severity, then call time, is how it is already sorted.
  4. Take calls as they come (10.3), routing them yourself rather than leaving them queued.
  5. Watch for automatic escalations — an unassigned call raises a High alert at 10 minutes and a blocking Critical at 20.
  6. Check Dispatch → KPI before handover so you hand over a described state, not a surprise.

11.3 Tasks

Route a call to departments
Screen /dispatch/pending or the incident  ·  Procedure route_incident()  ·  Full 22.2
  1. Open the incident from the queue.
  2. Select every department that needs to attend. A structure fire is usually Fire and EMS; add Police if a cordon is needed.
  3. Add dispatch notes if there is something the crew must know before arriving.
  4. Confirm.
Afterwards Status becomes Dispatched, dispatch_time is stamped, the timeline records who routed it, and every screen and phone in those departments is alerted. High and Critical open a blocking pop-up crews must acknowledge.
Assign a specific unit
Screen /dispatch/assign/[id]  ·  Procedure dispatch_unit()  ·  Full 22.3
  1. Open the incident from the queue and choose Assign.
  2. Review the map, the incident detail and the available units with their road-routed ETAs.
  3. Select the unit and confirm.
Afterwards The appliance leaves the available pool and the incident carries the unit. Routing tells a department; assigning commits an appliance — do both.
Send a message to a radio
Screen /dispatch/ptt  ·  Needs RealPTT configured  ·  Full 28.3
  1. Open the PTT console.
  2. Enter the destination account and the message text.
  3. Send. The two-hour audio log is on the same screen.

11.4 Not yours

Administration (/admin), departmental report capture, and HR approvals. For a role or fleet change, go to an administrator; for leave decisions, a supervisor.

Chapter 12

Dispatcher

Dispatcher

The dispatch desk only. Works the queue, allocates units, watches the board. Does not take calls.

12.1 Reach

The dispatch desk (/dispatch/pending, /dispatch/assign/[id], /dispatch/kpi, /dispatch/ptt), incidents, reports, case management, cameras, hierarchy, roster allocation, leave, own performance. Not /dispatch/log-call — intake is the call-taker's desk.

Why you cannot log a call The two-desk split matches how the control room is staffed and keeps the operational KPIs meaningful: call-taker throughput and dispatcher throughput are separate measures, and they stop being measures if one person does both under one role.

12.2 Shift routine

  1. Sign in; clear the alert bell.
  2. Dispatch → Pending Queue is your screen for the shift. Leave it open — it is live.
  3. For each call: route to departments, then assign a unit.
  4. Watch the dashboard board for crews that have gone quiet — the engine will flag no-arrival after 30 minutes and silence after 4 hours, but you will usually see it first.
  5. Use the PTT console rather than a personal handset, so the traffic is logged.
  6. At handover, walk the incoming dispatcher through anything still open.

12.3 Tasks

Routing (11.3), assigning (11.3) and PTT (11.3) as above, plus:

Respond to an SLA escalation
Trigger automation rule sla_unassigned  ·  See 37.2
  1. A High alert names the incident and how long it has waited. At the critical tier it opens a blocking pop-up.
  2. Open the incident from the alert.
  3. Either assign it, or record in the timeline why it cannot be assigned — no available unit, awaiting mutual aid.
Note The alert fires once per tier, not repeatedly. If you see the same incident escalate twice it has crossed from warn to critical, which is a real deterioration.
Handle an officer-emergency
Trigger panic from the responder app  ·  See Chapter 32
  1. A Critical blocking alert opens naming the officer. Acknowledge it.
  2. A red banner stays at the top of every page until the incident is closed — that is your standing reminder.
  3. Open the incident. The address is the officer's coordinates, or says plainly that the position is unknown.
  4. Assign the nearest units. Raise it on the radio in parallel; do not rely on the platform alone.
  5. Close the incident only once contact is made, with a note recording the outcome.
If it was accidental Close it as any other incident, with a note. The button is deliberately easy to trigger — an acknowledged false alarm is a far better failure than hesitation.
Chapter 13

Field Responder

Responder

The generic field role. Attends incidents, records what happened. Most crews will hold a departmental role instead (Chapters 14–17), which adds that department's reports.

13.1 Reach

/responder, /shift/*, /incidents, /reports, /roster/mine, /roster/allocation, /leave, /training, /performance/mine, plus the responder app. Responder is additionally admitted to all three departmental sections, which is unusual — most field roles get only their own.

13.2 Shift routine

  1. Complete start of shift before anything else (13.3). You cannot take incidents until it is done, on the phone or the console.
  2. Go on duty on the phone's Profile tab. This starts the GPS heartbeat so dispatch can see you.
  3. Work assigned incidents from the Incidents tab.
  4. Record evidence at the scene while you are there.
  5. Before signing out, check the sync tray is empty.
  6. Sign out from the phone — this releases your vehicle and clears your duty status.

13.3 Tasks

Start of shift
Screen Shift tab, or /shift/mental-check  ·  Writes mental_checks, apparatus, vehicle_checklists  ·  Full 24.1
  1. Mental readiness. Answer all six honestly: memory recall, judgment, situational awareness, not under the influence, anger check, reflexes. Add comments if anything is borderline.
  2. Vehicle selection. Choose your appliance from those available in your department.
  3. Vehicle inspection. Record fuel level (¼, ½, ¾, Full) and confirm lights, tyres, equipment stock, communications and brakes.
Why it gates everything The mental check is a fitness-for-duty declaration and the inspection is a roadworthiness record. Attending a call with neither on file means there is no record you were fit to attend — which is the record that matters if the call goes wrong.
Advance an incident status
Screen phone Incidents tab, or /responder  ·  Procedure update_responder_status()  ·  Full 23.1
  1. Open the incident.
  2. Press the single forward button — it always shows the next status: Mark En Route, then Arrived, On Scene, Transporting, Resolved.
  3. Press it when the thing actually happens, not when you next have a moment.
With no signal The card changes immediately and shows Waiting to send. The time recorded is when you pressed it, not when signal returned. Do not press it again.
Record scene evidence
Screen phone incident detail  ·  Writes incident_attachments, storage  ·  Full 30.4
  1. Open the incident, scroll to Scene evidence.
  2. Camera to take a photograph, Library to attach an existing one, or Record voice note for a spoken account.
  3. Capture what you need. Coverage does not matter — files are stored on the phone and upload themselves.
Afterwards Attachments appear in the case file and in the case-report PDF. A photograph of a scene cannot be retaken, so capture generously.
Add a timeline update
Screen phone incident detail, or /incidents/[id]
  1. Type into the update box and send.
Use for Anything the record should carry that a status cannot express: what you found, what you did, who else attended, why something took the time it did.

13.4 Not yours

Routing and assigning, closing cases, approving leave, administration. If you need a call sent to another department, tell dispatch — do not try to route it yourself.

Chapter 14

Metro Police Officer

Police · Officer · Metro · Security · Captain

Metro Police field roles. Everything in Chapter 13, plus the police report suite. Captain additionally holds supervisory reach.

14.1 Reach

Field responder reach, plus /police and everything under it — reports, firearms, security (SIRA) reports, crime hotspots. Restricted to the MetroPolice silo: an officer in another department cannot open it even with the same role value.

14.2 Tasks

File a police case report
Screen /police/reports/new  ·  Writes metro_reports  ·  Full 25.1
  1. Open Police → Reports → New.
  2. Head the report: case number, date and time of offence, location, station, incident category.
  3. Record yourself: officer name, badge number, partner name, and whether bodycam was active.
  4. Record the people: suspect name and description, offender name and ID, victim name, ID, contact and injuries.
  5. Record the outcome: arrest made, charges, use of force, fine amount or ticket number.
  6. Record property: vehicle details and impound, weapon type and size, ammunition rounds, contraband, and the evidence log (weapons, drugs, property).
  7. Write the officer narrative — the account that will be read in court.
  8. Submit.
Afterwards The report is filed against your department and appears in the archive and in Report Hub exports. Link it to an incident where one exists.
Issue a firearm authorization
Screen /police/firearms/new  ·  Writes firearm_authorizations
  1. Open Police → Firearms → New.
  2. Record the officer, the firearm and the authorization period.
  3. Submit.
On return Use Return against the register entry. An unreturned authorization is a live accountability gap.
Capture a SIRA security-guard report
Screen /police/security/new  ·  Writes security_reports
  1. Open Police → Security → New.
  2. Record the guard: name, guard ID, badge, contact, ID number, SIRA number and grade.
  3. Record whether a firearm was issued and its number.
  4. Record GPS arrival and departure times and the site.
  5. Submit.
To close it Use Resolve and enter a resolution summary.
Check crime hotspots
Screen /police/crime-hotspots  ·  Reads crime_stats
  1. Open Police → Crime Hotspots.
  2. Filter by station and category. The dataset is the SAPS statistics, ranked.
Use for Deployment planning and briefings, not for live response.
Chapter 15

Firefighter

Firefighter · Chief · FireChief

Fire & Rescue field roles. Chapter 13, plus the fire report suite, the hydrant register and post-incident review. Chief and FireChief additionally hold supervisory reach.

15.1 Reach

Field responder reach, plus /fire — reports, hydrants, PIR. Restricted to the MetroFire silo.

15.2 Tasks

File a fire incident report
Screen /fire/reports/new  ·  Writes firefighter_reports  ·  Full 25.2
  1. Open Fire & Rescue → Reports → New.
  2. Head it: report number, date and time of incident, location, type of incident, station number, fire department name, and the reporting officer name, rank and ID.
  3. Record the timings in minutes: dispatch, arrival, clear, total. These feed the response-time measures.
  4. Record the property: type, owner, contact, value, contents value, insurance, damage estimate.
  5. Record the fire: point of origin (room and object), ignition source, cause, initial observations, smoke conditions, flame spread, heat signature.
  6. Record the response: suppression details, equipment used, ventilation, salvage and overhaul, utilities secured, search and rescue, persons rescued with location and condition.
  7. Record casualties separately for civilians and firefighters — injuries and fatalities, each with detail.
  8. Record hazmat operations, hazards identified, medical aid provided, treatment, mutual aid and other agencies on scene.
  9. Close with the narrative summary, challenges faced, successes, recommendations, and whether a fire investigation or post-incident critique is to follow.
  10. Submit.
Afterwards Download the PDF from the report page for the station file. If a critique is scheduled, the PIR flow follows.
Publish a post-incident review
Screen /fire/pir · /fire/pir/publish/[reportId]  ·  Writes public_incident_reports
  1. Open Fire & Rescue → PIR and choose the report.
  2. Review the content that will be published.
  3. Publish.
Care This makes a summary publicly visible. Check names and addresses before publishing.
Use the hydrant register
Screen /fire/hydrants  ·  Reads hydrants
  1. Open Fire & Rescue → Hydrants.
  2. Find the incident location on the map; hydrants render as markers with their detail.
Use for Pre-planning and live water supply. This is operational data during a fire, not a maintenance list.
Chapter 16

Paramedic

Paramedic · Doctor

EMS field roles. Chapter 13, plus the patient care record and ambulance restocking.

16.1 Reach

Field responder reach, plus /medical — PCR create, view and edit, and restock. Restricted to the Medical silo.

16.2 Tasks

Complete a patient care record
Screen /medical/pcr/new  ·  Writes paramedic_reports and its child tables  ·  Full 25.3
  1. Open EMS → PCR → New.
  2. Patient: name, date of birth, gender, address, contact, insurance, next of kin, allergies.
  3. History: presenting complaint and relevant medical history.
  4. Assessment: your findings.
  5. Vital signs: record each set with its time. Multiple sets are expected on a transport.
  6. Interventions: what you did, and when.
  7. Handover: the receiving facility and the legal handover record.
  8. Signatures: capture responder, hospital representative and doctor on the signature pad.
  9. Submit.
Editing Use PCR → Edit to correct or complete a record. It remains linked to the incident, and every change is audited.
Record a restock
Screen /medical/restock  ·  Writes ambulance_restocks
  1. Open EMS → Restock.
  2. Select the unit, enter the drug name, quantity used and quantity remaining.
  3. Submit.
Do it at the end of each call, not at end of shift. Quantity remaining is what the next crew relies on.
Chapter 17

Disaster Management Officer

Disaster

Major incidents, floods, multi-agency response. Works over longer timescales than the other desks.

17.1 Reach

Field responder reach, plus /disaster — monitor, dispatch, partners, post-mortem. Restricted to the EthekwiniDisaster silo. Calls arrive as Pending Analysis rather than Pending Dispatch, because the first step is assessment.

17.2 Tasks

Dispatch resources to a disaster
Screen /disaster/dispatch  ·  Writes disaster_logs
  1. Open Disaster → Dispatch.
  2. Select the resources and the partners required.
  3. Confirm.
Post a periodic update
Screen /disaster  ·  Writes disaster_logs
  1. Find the log entry for the incident.
  2. Add the update text and submit.
Cadence matters A disaster log with hourly entries is a usable account afterwards; one with two entries is not. The engine will nudge after 4 hours of silence, but do not wait for it.
Write a post-mortem
Screen /disaster/post-mortem  ·  Writes post_mortem_reports
  1. Open Disaster → Post-mortem.
  2. Record what happened, what worked, what did not, and the recommendations.
  3. Submit.
Find a mutual-aid partner
Screen /disaster/partners  ·  Reads mutual_aid_partners
  1. Open Disaster → Partners.
  2. Filter by the resource you need. 41 partners are registered with their contacts and capabilities.
Chapter 18

Supervisor

Supervisor · Captain · Chief · FireChief

The supervisory tier. Runs people: rosters, duty allocation, leave, training, performance — and holds the dispatch desk too.

18.1 Reach

The widest non-administrative reach: /hr, /roster and all of it, /leave with decisions, /training, /performance in full, /analytics, /hierarchy, the dispatch desk, incidents, reports, cameras. Captain, Chief and FireChief also keep their departmental section from Chapters 14–16.

18.2 Routine

  1. HR Command Centre (/hr) first: headcount, on duty, on leave today, approvals waiting, certifications expiring within 30 days.
  2. Decide any pending leave — each request shows its coverage impact.
  3. Check the duty board for gaps before the shift starts.
  4. Chase unacknowledged allocations.
  5. Weekly: performance reports and the rollup.
  6. Annually from 1 July: appraisal plans.

18.3 Tasks

Decide a leave request
Screen /hr or /leave  ·  Writes leave_entries, alerts, audit_logs  ·  Full 26.3
  1. Open HR — pending requests are listed with an inline decision.
  2. Read the coverage impact: the percentage of the department away if you grant it.
  3. Approve or reject.
Afterwards The member gets a targeted alert immediately, and the decision is recorded against your name with a timestamp.
Allocate a duty
Screen /roster/allocation  ·  Writes rosters  ·  Full 26.2
  1. Open Roster → Allocation. The board is post-first: every post the shift must fill, with gaps visible.
  2. Assign a member to a post, setting vehicle, sector, call sign and any special instructions.
  3. Repeat until the gaps are closed.
  4. Publish the day. Until you publish, allocations are drafts and members cannot see them.
  5. Use Copy forward to seed the next day from this one.
Then chase acknowledgements. Publication makes it visible; acknowledgement is the accountability half. The engine reminds members 12 hours before the start.
Record training and certifications
Screen /training  ·  Writes training_records, certifications
  1. Open Training.
  2. For training: select the member, enter the training name, date completed and notes learned.
  3. For a certification: select the member, enter the name, expiry date, issued by, date achieved and remarks.
The expiry date is load-bearing. It drives automatic warnings at 30, 7 and 1 days to both the member and the department. A certification with no expiry recorded warns nobody.
Run the annual appraisal cycle
Screen /performance/*  ·  Writes performance_plans, performance_scores  ·  Full 26.5
  1. Define KPIs for the cycle year: title, description, weight, department.
  2. Generate plans for the cycle year — one per member.
  3. At mid-year, open each plan and save a score per KPI with comments and evidence.
  4. Approve the stage to lock it.
  5. Repeat at final.
Cycle The year runs from 1 July.
Set who reports to whom
Screen /hierarchy/person  ·  Writes reporting_lines
  1. Open Hierarchy and find the member.
  2. Set their reporting line.
Why it matters The performance rollup aggregates along these lines. A missing line means somebody's numbers do not reach their manager.
Chapter 19

Case Manager

CaseManager

Reviews and closes resolved incidents, and produces the official case file.

19.1 Reach

/case-manager and its audit view, /incidents, /reports, /report-hub, /hierarchy, /performance rollup and reports, /roster/allocation, /leave.

19.2 Tasks

Close a case
Screen /incidents/[id] or /case-manager  ·  Procedure close_case()  ·  Full 23.4
  1. Open the incident and read the timeline end to end.
  2. Check the departmental report exists where one is required.
  3. Check the attachments are present.
  4. Enter a reason and close.
Afterwards Status becomes Closed, closed_at is stamped, the reason goes into the timeline, and an audit row records you closed it. Closing is not reversible through the interface.
Produce the case-report PDF
Screen /incidents/[id]  ·  Endpoint /api/pdf/incident/[id]
  1. Open the incident and choose the PDF download.
  2. Wait — the document downloads and embeds every scene photograph, so it is the slowest request the platform serves.
Contents Incident detail, full timeline, embedded attachments and the audit trail. It is complete as a single document, which is what a case file has to be.
Review the audit trail
Screen /case-manager/audit  ·  Reads audit_logs
  1. Open Case Manager → Audit.
  2. Filter to the period or entity in question. Every mutation carries actor, department, action and detail.
Chapter 20

Staff and Viewer

Staff

Non-operational employees. Self-service only.

Reach: /dashboard, /profile, /profile/my-hr, /roster/mine, /roster/allocation, /leave, /reports/builder, /notifications, /search.

Tasks: apply for leave (26.3), view your schedule, keep your profile current, check your HR record.

Viewer

Read-only oversight. Sees across departments and changes nothing.

Reach: incidents, reports, report hub, case management, cameras, hierarchy, analytics, disaster, performance rollup and reports. Every screen is read-only — the role appears in is_global() but deliberately not in can_write_global().

Who this is for Oversight that needs the whole picture without the ability to alter it: senior command, auditors, a municipal oversight function. If somebody needs to change something, they need a different role, not an exception.
Chapter 21

Administrator

Admin · SystemAdmin · SuperAdmin

Runs the platform itself. Reaches every screen, including all of /admin. SystemAdmin and SuperAdmin additionally keep the left sidebar, because they genuinely work across every module at once.

21.1 Routine

  1. Admin → System Status daily. Each check says what it means rather than reporting a number.
  2. Admin → Automations — confirm the engine ticked and nothing is erroring.
  3. Weekly: integrations probe, fleet and device registers, audit spot-check.
  4. On joiners and leavers: create or retire accounts the same day.

Two things an administrator must understand
  • Role changes are silo-scoped in effect. Giving somebody Paramedic in the MetroPolice silo gives them a role that opens no departmental section, because /medical requires the Medical silo. Set both.
  • Disabling an automation is a real operational decision. Turning off unassigned-call escalation means nobody is told when a call ages. Do it knowingly, and turn it back on.
