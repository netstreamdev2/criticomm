Screen anatomy · The five zones of the intake screen — the numbered steps below walk them in order
Log an incoming call
Screen /dispatch/log-call  ·  Who Admin tier, ControlRoom, IncidentLogger  ·  Procedure log_incoming_call()
  1. Open the screen fresh for each call. An intake clock starts on open and is shown as Time since call intake started. It turns amber then red at the thresholds set in Operations Settings.
  2. Caller name and contact number.
  3. Call description / narrative — what is happening.
  4. Location. Either type into the address field and pick a suggestion, or click the map. Address, Latitude and Longitude stay in step automatically. Add Suburb, City and Postal code where known.
  5. Classification. Pick the incident category and sub-category from the configured tree, then Region (Central, North, South, West, Outer).
  6. Incident severity — Low, Medium, High or Critical. This decides whether receiving crews get a corner toast or a blocking interrupt.
  7. Casualties, if any: Number of victims (1–5, 6-10, 10+) and Medical severity code (P1 Red Immediate, P2 Yellow Urgent, P3 Green Delayed, P4 Blue Expectant). Tick EMS not required when it is plainly not a medical call.
  8. Internal dispatch notes — anything the crew must know before arriving.
  9. Check the duplicate warning. If it names an existing incident, open it rather than creating a second.
  10. Receiving departments, if you may route. Selecting none files the incident under your own department for the dispatch desk to route.
  11. Submit.
Afterwards An incident row exists with a sequential INC-YYYYMMDD-NNNN number, status Pending Dispatch (or Pending Analysis for Disaster Management), your name as call-taker, and an audit row. If you routed it: status Dispatched, alerts on every screen in those departments, and a push to their phones.

22.2 Route an incident to departments

/dispatch/pending
Take the oldest, worst call — the queue is already sorted that way.
SELECT DEPARTMENTS
Every service that must attend, plus notes the crews need before arriving.
route_incident()
Status → Dispatched · timeline entry · audit row · one transaction.
ALERTED
One alert per department. High and Critical open a blocking pop-up.
Flow · From the pending queue to every attending department alerted
Route an incident
Who Admin tier, Dispatcher, ControlRoom, Supervisor  ·  Procedure route_incident()
  1. Open the incident from Dispatch → Pending Queue.
  2. Select every department that must attend — more than one where the call needs it.
  3. Add notes if required.
  4. Confirm.
One transaction Status → Dispatched; dispatch_time stamped; dispatched_silos set (which is what grants those departments visibility); your name recorded as dispatcher; a timeline entry; an audit row; and one alert per department. It cannot half-succeed.

Assign a specific appliance
Screen /dispatch/assign/[id]  ·  Procedure dispatch_unit()
  1. From the queue, choose Assign on the incident.
  2. Review the incident detail, the map, and the available units with road-routed ETAs.
  3. Select a unit and confirm.
Afterwards The appliance leaves the available pool; the incident carries unit_id and unit_type. Routing tells a department; assigning commits an appliance.

22.4 Handle a duplicate

Check for a duplicate
Automatic on the log-call screen  ·  Procedure check_duplicate_incident()
  1. The check runs as you capture, over a 12-hour window, matching on caller number, and separately on location + incident type.
  2. If a match is shown, open it and confirm whether it is the same event.
  3. Same event — add what the new caller told you as a timeline update on the existing incident and do not submit a new one.
  4. Different event — continue and submit.
Why it matters Two incidents for one crash sends two crews and produces two partial records of the same event.

22.5 Request a citizen's GPS position

Send a location-request SMS
Screen /incidents/[id]  ·  Needs BulkSMS configured  ·  Writes citizen_share_tokens
  1. Open the incident.
  2. Enter the citizen's mobile number under Contact citizen.
  3. Submit. A single-use token is minted and an SMS sent with a link to /share-location/<token>.
  4. When they open it and allow location access, the position is written to the incident.
Security The public page authenticates by the token alone and can do nothing but post a position. Tokens expire and are purged.

Working an Incident

23.1 Advance the status

APP · INCIDENTS
Open your call. The forward button always names the next status.
TAP "MARK EN ROUTE"
Press it when the thing happens, not when you next have a moment.
OUTBOX
Written to the phone first, with the time you pressed it.
TIMELINE
The server records when you acted — even if delivery came later.
Flow · From a button press in the field to an honest timestamp in the record
Change an incident status
Screen /responder, /incidents/[id], or the phone  ·  Procedure update_responder_status()
  1. Open the incident.
  2. Press the forward button. It always names the next status in the ladder.
  3. Press it at the moment the thing happens.
Recorded The status, unit_status, and — on first arrival only — arrived_timestamp. A timeline entry is written. From the phone with no signal, the time recorded is when you pressed it, and the timeline says the update was delivered late.

Close an incident
Who Case manager tier and supervisors  ·  Procedure close_case()
  1. Read the timeline end to end.
  2. Confirm the departmental report exists where required, and that attachments are present.
  3. Enter the reason.
  4. Close.
Afterwards Status Closed, closed_at stamped, the reason in the timeline, and an audit row. Not reversible through the interface.

Start of Shift

24.1 The three-step gate

APP · SHIFT TAB
An unfinished flow routes you here on open — nothing else unlocks first.
MENTAL READINESS
Six declarations, answered honestly. Comments where borderline.
VEHICLE + INSPECTION
Choose the appliance, record fuel and the five checks.
GATE OPENS
Incidents unlock. The fitness and roadworthiness records now exist.
Flow · From opening the app to being cleared for incidents
Complete start of shift
Screens /shift/mental-check → /shift/vehicle-selection → /shift/vehicle-checklist, or the phone Shift tab  ·  Resets daily
  1. Mental readiness. Six declarations: memory recall, judgment, situational awareness, not under influence, anger check, reflexes. Add comments where anything is borderline.
  2. Vehicle selection. Choose an appliance from those available in your department. assign_vehicle() takes it out of the pool and attaches it to you.
  3. Vehicle inspection. Record fuel level — ¼, ½, ¾ or Full — and confirm lights functional, tyres condition OK, equipment stocked, comms working, brakes responsive.
Until all three exist for today the responder app routes you to the Shift tab and you cannot open incidents. Profile stays reachable so you can always see who you are signed in as and sign out.

24.2 Going on and off duty

Go on duty
Screen phone Profile tab  ·  Procedure toggle_duty()
  1. Open Profile and switch Duty status on.
  2. Allow location access when asked.
Afterwards A GPS heartbeat runs every 60 seconds so dispatch can see your position. It goes through the offline queue, so losing signal does not lose your position — the newest fix is delivered when you reconnect.
End of shift
Procedure cleanup_on_logout()
  1. Check the sync tray is empty. If anything is waiting and you have signal, give it a moment.
  2. Sign out from Profile.
Afterwards Your vehicle is released, duty is cleared, and your push registration is removed so the next person carrying the handset does not receive your alerts.

Chapter 25

Departmental Reports

Each department captures its own report against the shared incident. Full field lists appear in the role playbooks (Chapters 14–16); this chapter covers what is common and what to watch.

25.1 Police case report

Screen /police/reports/new · Writes metro_reports. Seven capture areas: heading, officer, people, outcome, property and evidence, narrative. See 14.2 for the field-by-field procedure.

The narrative is the report Every other field is structured data for searching and statistics. The officer narrative is what gets read in court. Write it as an account, not as notes.

25.2 Fire incident report

Screen /fire/reports/new · Writes firefighter_reports. Ten capture areas including timings, property, fire behaviour, response, casualties, hazmat and outcome. See 15.2.

Timings feed the measures Dispatch, arrival, clear and total are recorded in minutes and are what the fire response-time measures are computed from. Estimate honestly rather than leaving them at zero.

PDF: download from the report page — /api/pdf/fire-report/[id].

25.3 Patient care record

Screen /medical/pcr/new · Writes paramedic_reports plus medical_histories, assessment_findings, vital_signs, interventions, legal_handovers. See 16.2.

Signatures Responder, hospital representative and doctor sign on the canvas. The handover is a legal transfer of care; an unsigned PCR is an incomplete record of who was responsible for the patient and when.

25.4 Security and firearms

SIRA guard reports at /police/security/new with a Resolve flow requiring a resolution summary; firearm authorizations at /police/firearms/new with a Return action. Both are registers — the value is in them being closed out, not just opened.

26.2 Duty allocation

/roster/allocation
The board shows every post and its gaps for the day.
ASSIGN
Member → post, with vehicle, sector, call sign and instructions.
PUBLISH DAY
Drafts become visible to members — not before.
ACKNOWLEDGED
Members confirm from My Schedule; the engine nudges at T-12h.
Flow · From an empty board to an acknowledged duty day
Fill and publish a duty day
Screen /roster/allocation  ·  Writes rosters, duty_posts
  1. Open Roster → Allocation. The board lists every post the shift must fill, with its minimum staffing, and shows the gaps.
  2. Assign a member to a post: set badge, station, activity, vehicle, sector, call sign and instructions.
  3. Repeat until covered. Unassign to correct a mistake.
  4. Publish day. Drafts are invisible to members until this.
  5. Copy forward to seed another day from this one.
Then Members acknowledge their allocation from My Schedule. The engine reminds anyone who has not, 12 hours before the start.
Maintain the establishment
Screen /roster/allocation  ·  Writes duty_posts
  1. Use Save post to create or edit a post: name, minimum staff, station, sector, vehicle, call sign, activity and sort order.
  2. Use Retire post when a post no longer exists. Retiring rather than deleting preserves history.
Print the duty sheet
Screen /roster/duty-sheet
  1. Open Roster → Duty Sheet and choose the day.
  2. Print. The layout carries shift blocks, the on-leave list, special events and sign-off columns.

26.5 Performance

Run the appraisal cycle
Screens /performance/kpis · /performance/plans  ·  Who Supervisor and admin tier
  1. Add KPIs for the cycle year: department, title, description, weight.
  2. Generate plans for the cycle year — one per member.
  3. Open a plan and save a score per KPI: stage (mid-year or final), score, comments, evidence.
  4. Approve the stage to lock it.
Retiring a KPI Use Deactivate rather than deleting, so historical scores remain interpretable.
Read performance
Screens /performance/operational · /mine · /rollup · /reports · /role-kpis
  1. Operational — call-taker and dispatcher throughput, category pivot, call detail, logons, with CSV export.
  2. Mine — your own throughput against the team, monthly trend, appraisal snapshot.
  3. Rollup — the same measures aggregated up the reporting hierarchy.
  4. Reports — one date window by member, by department and by incident type, each with CSV.
Chapter 27

Reporting and Analysis

27.1 Report Hub

Build and save a filtered incident report
Screen /report-hub  ·  Writes command_records (ReportTemplate)
  1. Set the filters: free-text search, status, department, category, region, and a from/to date window.
  2. Review the result.
  3. Export CSV, or save as a template with a name so the same cut can be repeated.

27.2 Report Builder

Build a custom report
Screen /reports/builder  ·  Writes report_definitions
  1. Choose a dataset from the registry. Only datasets your role may query are offered.
  2. Add filters. Fields, operators and enum values are validated against the registry.
  3. Choose grouping and a chart type.
  4. Run.
  5. Save with a name, description and visibility, or export CSV.
Why filters get dropped silently A filter naming a column that is not in the registry, an operator the field type does not support, or an enum value that does not exist is discarded rather than passed through to the database. This is the defence that keeps a user-composed query from becoming a way to read something it should not.

27.4 Search

Find a record from any string
Screens ⌘K anywhere, or /search  ·  Procedure search_everything()
  1. Press ⌘K / Ctrl+K, or open Search.
  2. Type at least two characters. Partial identifiers work — part of an incident number, a plate, a badge, a surname.
  3. Results group by kind: incidents, personnel, fleet, police reports, fire reports, security reports.
Scope Results are RLS-scoped, so you see only what you could open anyway. The /search page keeps the query in the URL, so a search can be bookmarked or sent to a colleague.
Chapter 28

Body Cams, Cameras and External Systems

28.1 Watching a bodycam live

Watch an officer's bodycam
Screen /cameras (Body Cams), the live map, or the incident  ·  Endpoint /api/bodycams/view  ·  Audited
  1. Open Body Cams and choose the officer, or click an officer's red dot on the live map. From an incident, use Watch live.
  2. Say why: officer safety, SOS, supervising, investigation, or other with a note. From an incident, the incident is the reason.
  3. Watch up to four at once. After ten minutes the feed asks Still watching? and stops a minute later if nobody answers.
On the record Every view is recorded with your name, the bodycam, the officer and the reason, and is in the audit log. The officer's phone shows that the control room is viewing their camera, never who.

28.2 Body cam footage

Find and play recorded footage
Screen /cameras/footage (Body Cam Footage)  ·  Endpoint /api/bodycams/view  ·  Audited
  1. Open Body Cam Footage, or Footage for this incident on an incident.
  2. Choose the window and, if you like, the officer. The bookings show who had which bodycam, and when.
  3. Choose a booking, say why, and play it. Windows are capped at 24 hours.
Chain of custody Every playback is recorded with your name and reason. Footage retrieval is evidence handling, and who requested what has to be answerable later.

28.3 SANRAL freeway cameras

View the freeway cameras
Screen /sanral (SANRAL Cameras)  ·  Endpoint /api/cctv/cameras
  1. Open SANRAL Cameras. Each camera opens its feed page; the registry is imported from i-traffic.
  2. The same cameras, and live traffic, are layers on the live map.

28.4 Radio (PTT)

Send text to a radio
Screen /dispatch/ptt  ·  Who Dispatch roles  ·  Rate limit 60/min
  1. Open Dispatch → PTT.
  2. Enter the destination account and the message.
  3. Send. Your name is derived from your profile and attached as the sender.
  4. The two-hour audio log is on the same screen.
