Chapter 45

The System Controller

SystemController

The command-and-reporting seat. Watches every department at once, runs the operational modules, and answers for the shape of the day — without holding the keys to accounts, tenants or the platform's own switches.

This role was added with the command platform. It exists because there was a gap between a Supervisor — who runs one department's people — and the admin tier, whose powers include creating accounts and reshaping the platform itself. A controller needs the first without the second.

45.1 Reach

ScreenUse

/dispatch/* · /incidents/*Every department's calls, live.
/duty-tasksStation tasks — create, assign, chase.
/attendanceThe clock register for the whole silo.
/mailInternal messages.
/police/fines · charges · transactions · warrants · summonsThe enforcement paper trail.
/police/firearm-checksArmoury checklists from the field, read-only.

What a controller cannot reach /admin account writes (creating users, changing roles), /admin/tenants, /admin/features and /admin/access. Those are the platform tier, not the command desk. A controller who needs a new account or a module switched on asks a SuperAdmin — and that request leaves a trail on both sides, which is the point.

45.2 Shift routine

  1. Sign in and open Command. Read the three tiles first: strength on duty, leave awaiting a decision, and calls past their response target.
  2. Clear the leave queue. Anything marked urgent starts within seven days and blocks somebody's planning.
  3. Read the allocation panel shift block by shift block. Pending against a name means the member has not acknowledged; Absent means they were expected and are not there.
  4. Check the coverage bar against the establishment. A number well under strength is the day's first problem, not the afternoon's.
  5. Work the duty-maintenance card — anything overdue is chased now.
  6. Through the day, keep the command screen open. It is the only screen that shows every department at once.
  7. Before handover, confirm nothing is left breaching, no leave decision is outstanding, and any task you could not close has a note on it.

45.3 Tasks

Read the command dashboard
Screen /command  ·  Who Supervisory tier, SystemController  ·  Reads profiles, rosters, leave_entries, duty_tasks
  1. Responders on duty counts profiles currently flagged on duty against total personnel in your scope.
  2. Leave for approval splits pending from urgent, where urgent means the leave starts within seven days.
  3. Duty allocation groups today's roster into shift blocks. Each member carries a state: Confirmed (acknowledged), On leave (approved leave overlapping today), Absent (marked absent on the roster), or Pending (allocated, not yet acknowledged).
  4. Allocation coverage is allocated members over total personnel, as a percentage and a bar.
  5. Duty maintenance lists open tasks with their progress, and totals overdue / in progress / pending / done.
  6. System modules at the foot is the launcher. It shows only what your role may open and what your organisation has switched on, so a tile you cannot see is a tile you would not be allowed through anyway.
Scope A controller and the global tier see every department; a supervisor sees their own. The board defaults to Metro Police for a seat carried under the global Ethekwini silo, because that is the busiest board — not because the others are hidden.
Approve or reject leave from the command screen
Screen /command  ·  Who Admin tier, Supervisor, SystemController  ·  Writes leave_entries, alerts, audit_logs
  1. Find the request in the Leave approvals card.
  2. Check the dates against the allocation panel — approving somebody out of a shift that is already short is the decision this screen exists to inform.
  3. Approve, or ✕ to reject.
Afterwards The entry's status changes with your name and the moment on it, the member gets an alert (and a push to their phone), and an audit row is written. Identical in every respect to deciding it from the Leave screen — same table, same trail.

Sections

A section is an operational area — the code that appears beside an officer's name on the duty board. They are configuration, not code: an organisation defines its own.

46.1 The founding set

CodeNameRegion
NRNorth RegionNorth
WInner WestWest
DRDurban CentralCentral
BBeachfrontCentral
CHChatsworthSouth
OROperational Response ServicesMetro-wide

Seeded for Metro Police in the founding tenant. A new tenant starts with none and defines its own.

Add a section
Screen /admin/sections  ·  Who Admin tier, SystemController  ·  Writes sections, audit_logs
  1. Code — one to six letters or digits, uppercase. Short, because it has to fit beside a name on a duty board.
  2. Name — what people actually call the area.
  3. Region, optionally.
  4. Department — the silo it belongs to.
  5. Add.
Codes are permanent. A code is stamped onto duty tasks and roster rows as text, so renaming one afterwards would leave history pointing at a code that no longer means the same thing. To take a section out of use, retire it — it stops being offered on new records and stays readable on old ones.
Retire or reactivate a section
Screen /admin/sections  ·  Who Admin tier, SystemController
  1. Find the section in the register.
  2. Retire, or Reactivate to bring it back.
Afterwards Retired sections disappear from the pickers on new records. Nothing already stamped with the code changes.

Duty Maintenance

The standing work of a station that is not a call: vehicle inspections, CCTV checks, radio calibration, first-aid restocks. Numbered DM-NNNN, owned by a person, and due on a date.

/duty-tasks
Supervisor schedules the task — title, owner, unit, section, due date.
next_doc_number('DM')
The task takes the next DM- number for this organisation.
ALERT
The assignee is told at once — console alert and push.
PROGRESS
The owner advances it. Any progress above zero moves it to In Progress.
COMPLETED
Progress pins to 100 and the completion moment is stamped.
Flow · From scheduled to closed, with the owner able to move it themselves
Schedule a duty task
Screen /duty-tasks  ·  Who Supervisory tier, SystemController  ·  Writes duty_tasks, alerts, audit_logs
  1. Task — what has to be done, phrased so the person doing it needs no further explanation.
  2. Department, if you run more than one board.
  3. Assignee. Leaving it unassigned is allowed and means nobody has been told.
  4. Unit / apparatus and section, where the task belongs to one.
  5. Due date.
  6. Detail, if the title cannot carry it.
  7. Create task.
Afterwards The task exists as DM-NNNN at Pending, 0% progress. An assignee is alerted immediately, phone included. The task appears on the command dashboard's maintenance card and counts toward its totals.
Advance a duty task
Screen /duty-tasks  ·  Who Supervisory tier and SystemController for any task; the assignee for their own
  1. Find the task. Overdue rows carry the date in red.
  2. Set progress in tens, and the status.
  3. Save.
Rules the screen applies for you Progress above zero with no status chosen moves the task to In Progress. Choosing Completed pins progress to 100 and stamps the moment — you cannot record a task as complete and half-done at the same time. Only a supervisor may Cancel.
Why an assignee may move their own task The alternative is a supervisor transcribing progress reported over a radio, which is slower and less accurate than the person holding the spanner typing it. Row-level security allows exactly this and nothing wider: an assignee may update the task assigned to them, and no other.
Chapter 48

Time and Attendance

A clock register. Deliberately the simplest module on the platform, and deliberately the least editable.

The register is append-only There is no policy that permits updating or deleting an attendance event — not for a supervisor, not for an administrator. An attendance record that can be edited after the fact is not an attendance record. Corrections are new punches, and they are visible as corrections.
Clock in or out
Screen /attendance  ·  Who Anybody signed in  ·  Writes attendance_events, audit_logs
  1. Open Time & Attendance. The chip beside the button tells you what the platform currently believes: on the clock since a time, or off.
  2. Press Clock in or Clock out.
Double punches are refused If your last event was a clock-in, another clock-in does nothing. The guard sits in the action rather than the table, because the table is append-only by design and cannot express "no two of these in a row".
Read the register
Screen /attendance  ·  Who Own record: anybody. Whole silo: supervisory tier, SystemController, global tier
  1. Hours this week pairs each person's ins with their outs and totals the spans. Somebody still on the clock accrues up to now, and is marked On duty.
  2. Register lists every punch, newest first, with its source — console or mobile.
An unmatched in counts to the present moment rather than being discarded. Somebody who forgot to clock out shows an implausible total, which is the correct outcome: it is visible, and it is theirs to explain.
Chapter 49

Internal Mail

Operator-to-operator messages that never leave the platform — which is exactly why they exist. A message here is inside row-level security and on the audit trail; the same message on e-mail is neither.

Send a message
Screen /mail  ·  Who Anybody signed in  ·  Writes mail_messages, alerts, audit_logs
  1. Pick the recipient, write a subject and a message.
  2. Send.
Afterwards The message appears in their inbox immediately, and they also receive a low-priority alert — so it reaches a phone without pretending to be an emergency. You can see when they read it.
Read and mark a message
Screen /mail  ·  Who Sender and recipient only
  1. Unread messages carry an accent border in the Inbox.
  2. Mark read when you have dealt with it.
Who can see it Only the two people on it. Not their supervisor, not an administrator, not the global tier. Mail is the one module on the platform where the admin tier has no elevated read — a message people believe is private has to actually be private, or they will use something the platform cannot audit.

The Enforcement Suite

Metro Police paperwork: fines, charges, the money that moves against them, warrants and summonses. Five screens sharing one numbering scheme and one paper trail.

50.1 Document numbers

PrefixDocumentScreen
FN-Fine/police/fines
CH-Charge/police/charges
WA-Warrant/police/warrants
SM-Summons/police/summons
DM-Duty task/duty-tasks
These numbers never reset Incident numbers restart every day, because an incident is a thing that happened on a date. A fine number is quoted in a courtroom months later, so it is sequential per organisation and runs forever. Two organisations may each hold FN-0001 and they are different documents; within one organisation a number is issued once and never reissued.

50.2 Fines

Since 1 July 2026 eThekwini is under AARTO: a road traffic offence is served in person on an AARTO 01 infringement notice (50% discount if paid within 32 days). By-law offences stay Criminal Procedure Act matters: a Section 56 notice when the offender is present, or a Section 341 notice left on an unattended vehicle. Each charge gets its own FN- number. A served notice is a legal record: it is never edited or deleted, only cancelled with a reason. Full detail: docs/14-FINES.md.

Issue a fine at the roadside
Screen app → Home → Fines → Issue a fine  ·  Who Police, Captain, Supervisor  ·  Writes fines (through issue_fines), audit_logs
  1. Notice: AARTO 01, Section 56 or Section 341. Optionally the book number and the incident it belongs to.
  2. Infringer: surname, names, identification. A South African ID number is checked and fills in the date of birth and gender.
  3. Vehicle: registration, disc, type, make, model, colour.
  4. Offence: from the charge book (up to three charges), where it happened (Use my location), the court and date for Section 56, and the notes page.
  5. Sign: check the amount and pay-by date, tick that the notice was explained, sign, and have the infringer sign (or record a refusal). Issue fine.
Afterwards The slip opens: print it or share it as a PDF. With no signal it is a provisional slip (P-…); the notice sends itself when the phone finds signal and gets its FN- numbers.
Capture a notice from a paper book
Screen /police/fines/new  ·  Who Police, Captain, Supervisor  ·  Writes fines (through issue_fines), audit_logs
  1. Copy the notice exactly as written, with its printed notice number and when it was served.
  2. Capture notice. The signatures stay on the paper.
Record a payment, a representation or a cancellation
Screen /police/fines → open the fine  ·  Who Captain, Supervisor, Admin, SystemAdmin  ·  Writes charge_transactions, fines status, audit_logs
  1. Record money: payment, refund or adjustment, with the receipt reference. The ledger sets Partially Paid or Paid itself; an AARTO fine paid at the discount within 32 days is paid in full.
  2. Representation: mark Disputed when one is lodged; set back to Unpaid if it fails.
  3. Cancel: for a notice issued in error, with the reason (at least 10 characters). Refund any money first. It stays on the register, marked cancelled.
The ledger is never edited. A mistake is put right with a refund or an adjustment, so what was banked and what was recorded can always be compared.
Keep the charge book
Screen /police/fines/offences  ·  Who Admin (Metro Police), SystemAdmin  ·  Writes fine_offences
  1. For each offence enter the code (AARTO Schedule 3 or by-law section), legal reference, penalty and demerit points.
  2. Tick Verified. Changing a verified code or amount takes the tick off until it is checked again.
When an offence has an amount, the notice charges that amount and the officer cannot change it.

50.3 Charges

Open a charge
Screen /police/charges  ·  Writes charges, audit_logs
  1. Accused name and ID number.
  2. Charge code, description, statute and severity — Minor, Serious or Severe.
  3. SAPS case reference, where one exists.
  4. Court and court date if already set.
  5. Open charge.
Afterwards CH-NNNN at Open, with you recorded as the officer.
Work a charge and its ledger
Screen /police/charges/[id]  ·  Writes charges, charge_transactions, audit_logs

  2. Disposition — move the status through Referred to Court, Withdrawn, Convicted or Acquitted, setting the court and date as they are known.
  3. Record a transaction for bail, court fees, payments, refunds or adjustments, with a receipt reference.
  4. The ledger lists everything recorded, with a net total. Refunds subtract.

50.4 Warrants and summonses

Register a warrant
Screen /police/warrants  ·  Writes warrants, audit_logs
  1. Subject name and ID number.
  2. Type — Arrest or Search.
  3. Issuing court, expiry date and address.
  4. Register.
Executing one Set the status to Executed from the register; the moment and your name are stamped onto it automatically. Cancelled and Expired are also available. Only active warrants offer the control.
Issue and serve a summons
Screen /police/summons  ·  Writes summonses, audit_logs

  2. Court, court date and the service address.
  3. Issue.
  4. When it is served, set the status to Served — the moment and the server's name are stamped for you.
Onward Court Date Set, then Concluded or Withdrawn. Concluded and withdrawn summonses stop offering the control.

50.5 The transactions register

Record a transaction against any document
Screen /police/transactions  ·  Writes charge_transactions, audit_logs
  1. Choose the charge or the fine it settles against.
  2. Type, amount, method and reference.
  3. Record.
A transaction must reference something. The database refuses a row that names neither a charge nor a fine — money with no document against it is not a record, it is a rumour.

Firearm Checklists

The armoury checklist an officer completes at the counter — on the phone, where the firearm is. The console reads them and never writes them.

AT THE COUNTER
Firearm issued, returned or inspected on parade.
PHONE · Firearm Check
Serial, make, the five safety items, magazines and rounds.
OUTBOX
Queued with a client-generated row id. Armouries have no signal.
DELIVERED
Stored with the time it was completed, not the time it was sent.
/police/firearm-checks
Read-only on the console, pass or fail computed by the database.
Flow · From the armoury counter to the register, through a basement with no coverage
Complete a firearm checklist
Screen Phone → Home → Firearm Check  ·  Who Metro Police, when the module is enabled  ·  Writes firearm_checklists
  1. Choose the type — Issue, Return or Inspection.
  2. Serial number (stored uppercase) and make / model.
  3. Work the five safety checks: serial verified against the register, barrel clear, action cycles, safety catch functional, sights secure. Then cleaned and oiled.
  4. Magazines and rounds. A Return also asks for rounds back.
  5. Defects or remarks — anything the armourer must know.
  6. Submit.
Pass is computed, not claimed. The database derives pass or fail from the five safety items as a generated column. An officer cannot record a pass with a box unticked, and no screen can be built that would let them. The form shows the same derivation live, so the result is never a surprise.
Offline and replay The checklist is queued through the outbox (Chapter 31) with a row id generated on the handset. If delivery is interrupted after the database committed but before the phone knew, the retry violates the primary key — and that violation is proof it already landed, so it is treated as success rather than filed twice. The stored time is when the officer stood at the counter.
Review the checklist register
Screen /police/firearm-checks  ·  Who Captain, Supervisor, admin tier, SystemController; an officer sees their own
  1. Each row shows the officer, the type, the firearm, the six checks as pass/fail chips, the ammunition counts and the result.
  2. Anything not passed is worth a conversation the same day.
Read-only, deliberately. A checklist is testimony from the person holding the weapon at that moment. Nobody amends it from a desk afterwards — a correction is a new checklist.
Chapter 52

Multi-Tenancy

One deployment carries many organisations. A municipality's data does not exist for another one — on any screen, in any export, in any search.

52.1 How isolation works

JWT
The signed-in session carries tenant_id in its app metadata.
current_tenant()
Reads it, falling back to the founding organisation for older sessions.
RESTRICTIVE POLICY
Every table carries one, ANDed onto whatever else permits the row.
SCOPED
Cross-tenant rows are not filtered out by the app — they are never returned.
Flow · Tenancy is enforced by the database on every query, not by the screens
Why the policy is RESTRICTIVE Ordinary row-level security policies are permissive: they OR together, so each one can only widen access. Tenancy added that way would have widened what people could see. A restrictive policy ANDs instead — every other rule still applies, and tenancy is layered on top as a condition none of them can override.

52.2 What is per-tenant

ThingBehaviour
Incident numbersRestart daily within an organisation. Two may both hold INC-…-0001.
Document numbersFN/CH/WA/SM/DM run per organisation and never reset.
Personnel, call signs, sectionsEntirely separate registers.
Settings, integrations, brandingKeyed per organisation.
Feature switches and access rulesPer organisation (Chapter 53).
SearchRuns under the caller's own permissions, so results are already scoped.
Create an organisation
Screen /admin/tenants  ·  Who SuperAdmin only  ·  Writes tenants, audit_logs
  1. Name — how it is displayed.
  2. Slug — lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, two to thirty-nine characters. Permanent once created.
  3. Create.
Afterwards The organisation exists and is active. It has no users until somebody is assigned to it.
Assign a user to an organisation
Screen /admin/tenants  ·  Who SuperAdmin only  ·  Writes auth metadata, profiles, audit_logs
  1. Enter the account's e-mail and pick the organisation.
  2. Reassign.
Takes effect at their next sign-in. The change is written into the account's token metadata, and a token already issued keeps its old organisation until it is replaced. Their profile row moves with them; their historical records do not — a person changes organisation, history stays where it happened.
Suspend or delete an organisation
Screen /admin/tenants  ·  Who SuperAdmin only
  1. Suspending is the reversible off-switch, and is what you almost always want.
  2. Deleting requires typing the organisation's slug to confirm. A click can be a slip; a typed slug cannot.
Two deletions are refused outright. The founding organisation, because every session without an explicit organisation resolves to it. And any organisation that still has users assigned — reassign or remove them first. Deleting clears that organisation's feature switches, access rules and document counters along with its register row.
Chapter 53

Feature Switches and Access Control

Two layers that shape a deployment without a code change. Both belong to the SuperAdmin, and neither can grant anybody anything.

53.1 The three gates

GateAnswersWhere it lives
Feature switchDoes this organisation have the capability at all?Per-tenant, stored as deviations from the catalogue
Route matrixMay this role open this screen?Code — the same matrix in Chapter 3
Access ruleHas the SuperAdmin hidden it here?Per-tenant, per-role, deny only

They compose with AND. A screen opens only when all three allow it.

Neither layer can grant A feature switch gates availability, never authorization — turning one on gives no role anything the route matrix and row-level security would have refused. Access rules subtract only: a stored row can remove a module, never add one. A mistaken or malicious row can therefore hide something, but it can never escalate anybody.

53.2 Feature switches

Turn a capability on or off for an organisation
Screen /admin/features  ·  Who SuperAdmin only  ·  Writes feature_flags, audit_logs
  1. Each switch names what turning it off does — that is the decision being made.
  2. Toggle it.
Afterwards The screens it covers disappear from navigation and refuse if reached directly; APIs it covers answer not found. Consoles pick the change up live. On the phone the change lands on next sign-in or foreground.
SwitchWhat OFF removes
web.commandThe command dashboard. Its modules stay reachable from the sidebar.
web.enforcementFines, charges, transactions, warrants and summonses — together, since they share one paper trail.
web.duty_maintenanceThe DM- task board. Existing tasks keep their numbers.
web.time_attendanceThe clock register, and clocking in or out.
web.mailInternal mail. Alerts and notifications are separate and keep working.
web.sectionsThe sections register. Codes already stamped on records still display.
web.assistant · web.cameras · web.ptt · web.search · web.reports_builderAs documented in Chapter 35.
mobile.firearm_checkThe armoury checklist leaves the phone. Console review remains.
mobile.finesOfficers cannot issue fines from the app and the Fines card leaves the home screen. Notices already queued still send; the console register is unaffected.
mobile.panic · assistant · leave · nearby · voice_notes · alerts_inboxAs documented in Chapter 35.
The catalogue is code; the database stores only deviations Shipping a new capability adds a catalogue entry with its default, and every organisation gets that default with no data migration. A row appears only when a SuperAdmin actually flips a switch — so the absence of a row means "as shipped", not "unknown".

53.3 Access control

Hide a module for an organisation or a role
Screen /admin/access  ·  Who SuperAdmin only  ·  Writes access_rules, audit_logs
  1. Pick the organisation across the top. One SuperAdmin administers every one from this screen.
  2. To hide a module from everybody there, toggle it from Visible to Hidden.
  3. To deny it to one role, pick the role and press Deny role. Existing denials show as chips; click one to restore it.
Afterwards The module leaves the sidebar, the top navigation, the command palette and the command dashboard's launcher, and the route itself refuses. Enforcement is cached for a minute at the edge, so a change takes effect everywhere within about that long.
Two safeguards The SuperAdmin is exempt from every access rule — the seat that writes the rules can never lock itself out of the screen that writes them. And the dashboard, the profile screen and the three platform-tier consoles cannot be hidden at all: every account needs somewhere to land and a way to sign out, and a gate with no exit is a trap rather than a control.
