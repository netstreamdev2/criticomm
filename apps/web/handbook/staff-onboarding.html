How staff get their accounts

Nobody signs themselves up. An administrator invites a person from the staff register, and the invitation fixes their role. The person then proves who they are, chooses their own password and accepts the terms. Until they do, nothing exists that can sign in.

The journey, start to finish

Five steps. The second only happens for the powerful roles.

  1. Invite

    An administrator picks the person from the staff register and chooses their role.

    SystemAdmin: any role. Department Admin: department roles in their own department.

  2. Approve

    For SystemAdmin, Admin and SystemController only. A second SystemAdmin approves before anything is sent.

    Same Approvals page as role changes.

  3. Deliver

    By email, by SMS, or as a printed activation code handed over in person.

    Works once. Expires after 72 hours.

  4. Accept

    The person types their badge number, chooses a password and accepts the POPIA notice.

    The account is created at this moment, not before.

  5. Start work

    Desk staff land on their dashboard. Field staff are shown how to get the app and sign in.

    Their history starts with who invited them, and why.

Steps 1 and 2

What the administrator sees

A new Invite someone button on Admin → Users, an Invitations list next to it, and invitations for powerful roles in the existing Approvals page.

Step 3

What the person receives

Three ways in, one rule for all of them: it works once, expires after 72 hours, and is useless without the person’s badge number.

Step 4

What the person does

Four short screens. Opening the link changes nothing; only the person’s own button presses do. The same steps run in the app for printed codes.

Officers without an email address

Not every officer has municipal email, so the badge number does the job an email address does elsewhere. They receive their invitation by SMS or a printed slip, and they sign in with their badge number and password, on the web and in the app. Behind the scenes their account carries an internal sign-in address that never receives mail.

If they forget their password, an administrator issues a fresh activation code from their account page. That is logged like everything else. Staff with email get the usual “forgot password” email instead.

Before go-live

Bringing eThekwini’s staff across

Staff already exist somewhere: in the current Criticomm system, in HR and payroll, in station lists. We bring the people across, never their passwords or their old access. Everyone activates their new account in the weeks before go-live, so on the day itself they simply sign in.

Why not copy the old accounts as they are?

  • The old system’s passwords sat behind a database password that was found written in its source code. We have to treat them as possibly exposed, and copying them would carry that risk across.
  • Old accounts hold old access. Some of their roles no longer exist, and administrator rights should be appointed deliberately rather than inherited.
  • A fresh activation gives every person three things the old system never asked for: a password only they know, a check against the staff register, and their acceptance of the POPIA notice.
  1. Day zeroonce

    The HikTech team creates its own platform account. eThekwini nominates its two SystemAdmins in writing, and the HikTech team invites them. The nomination letter is recorded as the reason.

  2. Weeks before go-livewhile the old system is still in use
    1. Import the staff register. A SystemAdmin uploads one staff list from HR, cross-checked against the current system. It is checked on screen, and nothing is saved until they confirm.
    2. Review roles. Each person gets a proposed role based on their old one (table below). Department Admins confirm their own department. Administrator roles are never carried over: they are invited fresh, with a second SystemAdmin’s approval.
    3. Pilot. The control room and one police station activate first, and we fix whatever they find.
    4. Activate in waves. Department by department. Desk staff by email. Field staff in a short session at shift briefing, with their supervisor present and an SMS or printed slip each. About two minutes per person.
  3. Go-liveone night

    The incident history moves across overnight, as set out in the migration runbook. Accounts are already active, so staff simply sign in. The old system stays available, read-only, for 90 days for reference.

  4. After go-liveevery month

    The register stays in step with HR. A fresh staff list shows new joiners to invite, and people who appear to have left, for an administrator to deactivate with a reason. From then on, every new person arrives the same way: by invitation.

Handling the staff list with care

Only what’s needed

Name, badge or force number, rank, department, station, work email and work cellphone, and optionally their role in the current system, to suggest their new one. No ID numbers, home addresses or salaries.

Read in the browser

The file is checked on the administrator’s own computer. Only the confirmed rows reach HikTech, and the file itself is never stored.

A record of every import

Who imported, when, how many people, the reason, and a fingerprint of the file, so the exact list can be matched later.

Importing grants nothing

It adds people to the register. Only an invitation, accepted by the person themselves, creates an account.

Safe to repeat

An updated list is matched by badge number and updates people in place. It never creates duplicates.

Leavers are checked, not guessed

Anyone missing from HR’s list is shown for review. An administrator decides, and deactivates with a reason.

Safety built in

What protects the municipality, and the person, at each step.

Opening the link changes nothing

Outlook’s link scanner opens links before people do. Ours only shows a page; the person’s own button presses do the work, so nothing gets used up by a machine.

The badge number is the key

An invitation sent to the wrong address is useless to whoever receives it, because they don’t know the badge number on the staff record.

Once, then gone

Every link and code works once and expires after 72 hours. Resending cancels the old one. Codes are stored scrambled, never as typed.

Guessing is stopped

Five wrong badge numbers or codes lock the invitation. Only an administrator can send a new one.

Two people for powerful roles

Invitations as SystemAdmin, Admin or SystemController wait for a second SystemAdmin before anything is sent.

Nobody else knows the password

The person sets it themselves. Administrators never set, see or reset it to a known value.

“This isn’t me”

Anyone who gets an invitation by mistake can report it. It closes at once and the administrator is told.

Every step recorded

Invited, approved, sent, opened, accepted, cancelled: each with who and when. The new account’s history starts with who invited them and why.

POPIA

Acceptance of the notice is recorded with its version and the time. Emails and SMS carry no badge, ID or personal numbers.

What we agreed

Agreed on 3 October 2026. The alternatives are kept so the reasons stay visible.

  1. HikTech sends its own invitation emails

    AgreedThrough a mail server: eThekwini’s own in production, a test sender until then. Email, SMS and printed codes then all work the same way, links can last 72 hours, and the wording is ours.

    Considered: Supabase’s built-in invitation email. Its links last a day at most, and it can’t share codes with SMS and printed slips.

  2. Invitations last 72 hours

    AgreedLong enough for shift workers who are off for a weekend, short enough that an unused link doesn’t linger.

  3. The badge number proves it’s them

    AgreedIt is already on the staff record.

    Considered: part of their ID number. That means holding more personal information, which POPIA asks us to avoid.

  4. Staff without email sign in with their badge number

    AgreedOn the web and in the app.

  5. Administrators issue printed codes

    AgreedSystemAdmins, and department Admins for their own department: the same people who can invite.

For the build team

Where it lives and what to build

The rules above are the specification. Every account must come through an invitation and the same rules as a role change.

This handbook

apps/web/handbook/staff-onboarding.html is the single source. Edit it there.

Administrators read it in the app at /admin/handbook/staff-onboarding. A private shared copy exists for people without an account.

Rules it builds on

supabase/migrations/0048_account_safety_rules.sql: two SystemAdmins, reasons, approvals, permanent history.

packages/db/src/roles.ts: rolesAssignableBy, needsSecondApproval, confirmWordFor.

BuiltInvitations
  • supabase/migrations/0049_account_invitations.sql: table account_invitations. A link or code is stored only as a keyed hash (HMAC-SHA256 with INVITATION_SECRET), with channel, expiry (INVITE_TTL_HOURS, default 72), wrong-attempt count, status, personnel link and the POPIA notice version accepted.
  • Administrators, through their own session:
    • create_account_invitation, using the same ladder and approval as change_account_role; approval runs through decide_account_request
    • issue_invitation_secret (send, resend, new slip: the old link or code stops working), cancel_account_invitation, record_invitation_delivery_failure
  • The invited person, through the server only (service role):
    • preview_invitation: changes nothing, returns minimal fields only
    • check_invitation_badge: checks the badge number, counts wrong tries, locks at five
    • complete_account_invitation: creates the profile so its history starts with the inviter and reason
    • report_invitation, and rate_limit_hit_key for per-address limits
  • Shared rules in packages/db/src/onboarding.ts, server code in apps/web/lib/onboarding/, queries in packages/api/src/admin/invitations.ts.
  • Screens: /admin/invitations, /admin/invitations/new, the public /invite/[token], /activate and /legal/popia; in the app, “I have an activation code” (apps/mobile/app/activate.tsx, through /api/onboarding/check and /api/onboarding/activate).
  • Badge sign-in uses the address {badge}@{organisation}.badge.internal, which never receives mail. The sign-in screens assume eThekwini for now.
BuiltStaff register
  • supabase/migrations/0050_staff_register_import.sql: the register gains work email and cellphone, a suggested and a confirmed role for invitation, the last staff list that named each person, and a leaving date with its reason. Table staff_imports keeps every import permanently: who, when, the reason, the file’s name and SHA-256 fingerprint, and the counts.
  • Importing, SystemAdmin only: start_staff_import, import_staff_rows (up to 1,000 rows a call, every row checked again, matched by badge number, a blank cell never erases, an account holder’s department never moves) and finish_staff_import. Importing never creates an account.
  • Role review: set_register_roles, for a SystemAdmin, or a department Admin in their own department. Administrator roles are never set in bulk. Leavers: mark_register_left (cancels open invitations) and restore_register_person.
  • A badge number belongs to one current person: new duplicates are refused, existing ones are listed for cleanup, and a unique index takes over once there are none.
  • The file is read in the browser by apps/web/lib/register/ (Excel and CSV, no third-party spreadsheet library) and checked by packages/db/src/register.ts.
  • Screens: /admin/personnel (the register: filters, role review, bulk invitations, leavers), /admin/personnel/import, /admin/personnel/template (the workbook for HR) and /admin/rollout.
Settings

SMTP_HOST, SMTP_PORT, SMTP_SECURE, SMTP_USER, SMTP_PASS, SMTP_FROM for email. BULKSMS_TOKEN_ID, BULKSMS_TOKEN_SECRET for SMS. INVITATION_SECRET: its own long random value in production; changing it voids every invitation not yet accepted. INVITE_TTL_HOURS, default 72. Server-side only, never in the browser or in git.

NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_URL (HTTPS) is the address in invitation links; EXPO_PUBLIC_APP_URL is the same address for the app. NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_DOWNLOAD_URL, optional, is shown to field staff once they activate.

A development server without a mail server shows the administrator the link to pass on. Production never does.
