A police call, from first ring to closed case

Follow one call through HikTech. A call logger takes it, the Metro Police dispatch desk sends it out, an officer accepts it on the phone, goes to the scene and files the report, and a case manager reads the report and closes the case.

Who is involved

Four people touch a police call. Each sees only what their job needs.

Console

Call logger

Answers the phone and logs the call: who is calling, where, and what happened. Chooses which departments must receive it.

Console

Police dispatcher

Sees the calls sent to the Metro Police desk and sends each one to officers on duty. Watches every step live.

Phone app

Police officer

Goes on duty, accepts the call, drives to the scene, and files the report from the phone.

Console

Case manager

Reads every finished report and closes the case. Nothing is closed without someone reading it.

The control room sees every department’s calls and can do the call logger’s and the dispatcher’s work. Supervisors and Captains see their department’s calls and can step in at any point.

The journey, start to finish

Seven steps. The first happens once a shift, before any call.

  1. Go on duty

    The officer completes the start-of-shift checks on the phone.

    Officer · app · once a shift

  2. Log the call

    The call logger fills in the call form and sends it to the Metro Police desk.

    Call logger · console

  3. Dispatch

    The dispatcher sends it to every officer on duty, or to one chosen officer.

    Dispatcher · console

  4. Accept

    The officer gets a pop-up, accepts, and follows the route to the scene.

    Officer · app

  5. At the scene

    The officer marks On Scene, and adds photos, voice notes and updates.

    Officer · app

  6. File the report

    The officer fills in the police forms the call needs and files them.

    Officer · app

  7. Close the case

    A case manager reads the report and closes the case.

    Case manager · console

What the call says about itself

Pending Dispatch→ Dispatched→ En Route→ On Scene→ Resolved→ Closed

Resolved means the report is in. Closed means a case manager has read it. Every change is recorded with who made it and when.

Step 1 · Officer · phone

Going on duty

Calls only reach officers who are on duty. An officer goes on duty by completing four checks, once a shift. The bar at the top of each screen shows where they are: Readiness, Unit, Inspection, Loadout.

Step 2 · Call logger · console

Logging the call

What and where come first: the type, the description and the place. Then the caller, and who must receive the call. Criti, at the top, says what to ask and fills in what it can. The call logger checks it and decides.

Only when someone is hurt

The EMS box, and in the Description window the question Is the caller the patient? with each patient’s own severity, appear only when EMS is on the call: a medical type, an injury ticked, or EMS chosen. A burglary has none of them. The EMS guide shows them.

Reading the incident number

MP-20261013-0938-0004 is the departments it was sent to, the date, the time it was sent, and the call of the day: Metro Police, 13 October 2026, 09:38, the 4th call that day. MP Metro Police, FD Fire, MD EMS, DM Disaster Management, always in that order and joined in pairs: MPFD-…, MPFD-MD-…. The number never changes, even if a department is added later. Calls from before keep their INC- numbers.

If something is missing

A normal send waits until the form is complete. The message says exactly what to fix, for example:

  • Choose the incident type.
  • Place the incident on the map: pick a search result or click the map.
  • Choose the department this call is for: who must receive it.

Details added to a call already sent can be added only by the person who logged it, or the control room. Once a crew has accepted the call, only the dispatcher changes its type; once a crew is on scene, only the dispatcher changes the place.

Step 3 · Police dispatcher · console

Dispatching

The Metro Police desk sees only the calls sent to it, newest first, and only its own officers on duty.

Step 4 · Officer · phone

Accepting the call

The phone sounds and shows the call over whatever is open. Three choices, and accepting is asked once to be sure.

  1. 1Accept incident: the officer takes the call (leads it, or joins as backup if someone already leads).
  2. 2Acknowledge: seen, without accepting. The call opens in the list.
  3. 3Navigate: straight to the route map.

Step 5 · Officer · phone, and the dispatcher

At the scene

One button always shows the next step. The dispatcher sees each step on the console the moment it is pressed.

  1. 1The next step, one button. Backup officers mark only En Route and On Scene; the lead files the report.
  2. 2Evidence: up to 10 photos from the camera or the library, and voice notes. Each lands on the call’s record.
  3. 3Updates appear on the timeline for the dispatcher at once.
  4. 4Where the crew is. Each box fills in with the real time the officer pressed it. The dispatcher can also mark a step when an officer radios it in.
  5. 5The timeline: every step, photo and note, with who and when. Nothing on it can be deleted.

Step 6 · Officer · phone

Filing the report

The police report is made of the same seven forms as the paper system. The officer ticks the ones this call needs; the screen then asks only for those.

  1. 1Seven police forms: 12A Arrest, CR1 Crash, VI Vehicle Inspection, 216E Use of Official Firearm, SANRAL Incident, 211A Tow In, and Dog Capture / Tackle. Repeating parts, such as each vehicle in a crash, have an Add button.
  2. 2Every report starts with the response (partner, bodycam, use of force) and ends with the outcome, evidence booked and the narrative.
  3. 3The narrative is required: at least 20 characters, in the officer’s own words.
  4. 4File. The report is permanent from this moment; it can’t be edited or deleted. The call becomes Resolved and the case manager is told. If other departments are also on the call, it is resolved once they have filed too.

Step 7 · Case manager · console

Closing the case

Every resolved call waits for a case manager, longest waiting first. Closing is the last step, and it is recorded with who closed it and when.

Other ways a call ends

Not every call ends with a report.

Hoax, duplicate or cancelled

The dispatch desk chooses Close without a report and must say why (at least 10 characters). Any officer on the way is told to stand down.

Another department is also on it

Each department works its own part: its own lead, its own report. Police filing does not close the call while Fire or EMS are still working; it is resolved when all have finished. A department not needed is marked Not needed with a reason.

An officer in danger

The SOS button on the phone raises a Critical call at the officer’s position. It interrupts the dispatch desks and command, and is never sent back to the officer who raised it.

When there is no signal

The app keeps working in basements, lifts and dead zones.

What still works

Accepting, every step, photos, voice notes, updates and filing the report. Each is recorded with the real time it was done.

When signal returns

Everything is sent in order, never twice. The tray at the top of the app shows what is still waiting to send.

The route

The phone keeps recording where the officer goes. The console map draws the offline stretch once the phone reconnects.
