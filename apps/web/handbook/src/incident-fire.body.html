A fire call, from first ring to closed case

Follow one fire call through HikTech. A call logger takes it and sends it to Fire & Rescue (and EMS, because someone is hurt). The Fire dispatch desk sends a crew, a firefighter accepts on the phone, the crew works the fire and files the fire report, and a case manager closes the case.

The journey, start to finish

Seven steps. The first happens once a shift, before any call.

  1. Sign on

    The firefighter completes five start-of-shift steps and signs onto an appliance.

    Firefighter · app · once a shift

  2. Log the call

    The call logger fills in the call form and sends it to Fire & Rescue and EMS.

    Call logger · console

  3. Dispatch

    The Fire desk sends it to every crew on duty, or to one firefighter.

    Fire dispatcher · console

  4. Accept

    The firefighter accepts and follows the route.

    Firefighter · app

  5. At the fire

    On Scene, Transporting if needed, photos and updates.

    Firefighter · app

  6. Fire report

    The lead firefighter files the fire report.

    Firefighter · app

  7. Close the case

    A case manager reads the reports and closes the case.

    Case manager · console

Step 1 · Firefighter · phone

Signing on to an appliance

Five steps, once a shift. A firefighter always signs onto an appliance: there is no going on duty without one.

Step 2 · Call logger · console

Logging the call

The same form as every call. For a fire, the incident type suggests Fire & Rescue. When someone is hurt, Criti adds EMS and the medical questions appear.

People trapped? Send it now

A High or Critical call can go to dispatch as soon as it has a type, a place on the map and a department, before the caller’s details are in. The Police guide shows it under An urgent call.

Step 3 · Fire dispatcher · console

Dispatching a crew

The Fire desk sees the calls sent to it and only Fire & Rescue crews on duty. The call stays in the Fire queue until Fire dispatches, whatever EMS does.

Step 4 · Firefighter · phone

Accepting the call

The same pop-up as every department: Accept incident, Acknowledge or Navigate. Accepting is asked once.

Step 5 · Firefighter · phone

At the fire

One button shows the next step. A fire crew can also mark Transporting, for example when the appliance moves casualties.

  1. 1File fire report appears once the lead is On Scene. Only the lead files it.
  2. 2Mark Transporting: optional, beside the report.
  3. 3Photos, voice notes and updates work exactly as for every crew, up to 10 photos per call, and all reach the dispatcher at once.

Step 6 · Firefighter · phone

The fire report

The report is numbered from the call (FR-FDMD-20261013-0951-0007). The times fill themselves in from the call; the firefighter writes what happened.

  1. 1Response timeline, synced from the call: dispatched, responding, arrived, and the total time on scene.
  2. 2Nine short sections: Incident details, Narrative, Conditions (smoke, flame spread, heat, hazards), Actions & rescue, Support operations (ventilation, hazmat, medical aid, utilities, investigation), Origin & cause, Property & damage (values in rand), Casualties, and Debrief. Required: the type, a summary of at least 20 characters, and the cause (or Under investigation).
  3. 3Photos taken on the call count; with none, the firefighter must say why.
  4. 4File. Fire’s part of the call is finished and the report is permanent. Injuries or deaths must be described before it files.

Step 7 · Case manager · console

Closing the case

Exactly as for every call: once every department on it has filed, the call is Resolved and waits for a case manager. They read the reports, evidence and history, add an optional note, and close it. Closing tells the crews.

The Fire Chief’s view

The Fire & Rescue dashboard shows live fire incidents, the latest fire reports, hydrants in service and public incident reports. Every fire report also appears on the call’s page and in the case PDF.

With EMS on the same call
